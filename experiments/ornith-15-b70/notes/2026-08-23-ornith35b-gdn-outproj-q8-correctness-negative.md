# Ornith 1.5 35B-A3B: GDN output-projection Q8 fusion

Date: 2026-08-23 EDT

Status: **closed correctness negative; never performance-tested**

## Motivation

The Qwen-derived recurrent path feeds the accepted fused GDN RMSNorm/SiLU
gate output into a Q4_K 4096→2048 projection in each of 30 recurrent layers.
The ordinary reordered MMVQ path rereads the 4,096 FP32 values in a separate
Q8_1 quantization kernel. A default-off candidate attempted to preserve the
FP32 graph output while emitting the identical reordered Q8_1 row from the
GDN producer, which would remove 30 launches per decoded token.

The matcher was limited to the exact named `ssm_norm`, `z`, and `ssm_out`
chain, Q4_K weight, one-token shape, sole-consumer closure, contiguous storage,
and one-device layout. Its poison door aborted exactly at
`linear_attn_out-0` on the first decoded recurrent evaluation.

## Correctness result

The same-final-binary control produced the accepted canonical SHA-256
`d25039a7a21fccc7eaf5f9414803f80c1e3b58cc900ad8034448df8edb57e38c`.
All candidate forms failed it:

| Variant | Transcript SHA-256 | Hits | Result |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| fused FP32 producer + fused Q8 emitter | `123013a31b283b7c7c3f5ee87a28c04567551d3ef3364e1c1f49fe72011ddd70` | 270 | severe divergence / early stop |
| fused producer, Q8 overwritten by stock quantizer | `b2580dd3d6b57e07fa8691184d623941c4ab9f411f36174a8780279233dc760b` | 3,810 | non-exact producer |
| accepted producer loop, global barrier, stock Q8 overwrite | `8d6da0d6d5767e5d9f58b54adaf874861b6de715e675b9d168b1ed3a4827bc40` | 3,810 | still non-exact |

The stock-Q8 overwrite isolated the first emitter bug from a second problem:
placing the producer arithmetic in the larger kernel changed generation even
when the downstream Q8 data was regenerated by the established quantizer.
The global-memory-barrier form retained the accepted producer loop text and
reread its materialized graph buffer, but it still did not reproduce the
oracle. That exhausts the bounded, launch-saving form without relaxing the
lab's byte-exact gate.
