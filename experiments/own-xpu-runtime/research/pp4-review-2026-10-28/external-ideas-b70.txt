B70 external optimization ideas: primary-source scan, hypotheses only

No external performance figure below is our measurement or transferable to
SC117. No software installed, weights downloaded, device requests issued or
live/pinned sources changed. Current timing/state gate goes first. Preserve
full 16-bit KV and existing target arithmetic; do not train a draft. External
projects are research inputs, not a replacement base for our own runtime.

1. B70 small-batch XMX/ESIMD kernels for speculative verification.
Primary: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29864
Regression: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/31068
Merged commit: d81d0d8fb95e52a66992bcfc212b1891b4d2f94a.
Concrete idea: unpack each weight once across several activation columns;
int8 DPAS, 2D loads, batch-dependent dispatch and fused gate/up epilogues.
Relevant to 2-8-row target verification and short prompts, rather than simply
increasing draft depth. B70 is actually tested by the author. Important limits:
IQ formats and MoE MUL_MAT_ID were not the original target. Author reports
floating-summation changes and eventual greedy divergence. A separate B70 MoE
report finds large-prompt regression. Read kernel layout/dispatch, do not port
arithmetic blindly. Minimal gate: existing real production shapes at M=1/2/3/
4/8, exact intermediate/state comparison, then matching verification timing.

2. Full-precision attention: split-KV occupancy plus Intel matrix paths.
Primary: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-xpu-kernels
Primary: https://github.com/intel/sycl-tla/blob/main/CHANGELOG-SYCL.md
Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135
Concrete idea: separately tune small-query long-KV work distribution and large
prefill tiling. Intel's kernel library includes Xe2/GDN/flash attention; SYCL-TLA
has Xe copy/MMA and grouped-GEMM primitives. Use 16-bit KV while examining
coalesced loads, prefetch and subgroup work distribution. Mathematical exact
attention does not guarantee identical FP reduction order: a split/combine
rewrite must pass our bitwise oracle. Native QSA selection is not generic dense
attention; preserve its exact selected indices and order. Local attention-plus-
combine epilogue already lost despite exactness: research/rank0-attention-fuse-
c27/RESULT.md. Minimal gate: isolate long-context attention shape and keep the
existing reduction order before attempting fusion or repartitioning.

3. Group expert slots by expert, then specialize tile occupancy by shape.
Primary: https://github.com/alpha2beta/vllm.xpu
Primary: https://github.com/sybil-solutions/exl3xpu
Concrete idea: single grouped expert projection launches, multi-row vectors,
decode/GEMM crossover dispatch and expert-route bucketing. The alpha2beta work
is on XMX-less Arc140V, not B70, so its no-DPAS choice should not carry across;
its routing/launch structure is the useful part. EXL3 is a different weight
format, not an SC117 speed comparison. Its repo distinguishes bit-identical
weight reconstruction from non-identical end-to-end logits, and its optional
INT8 prefill is lossy. Local exact xgroup already won and is enabled:
research/prefill-experts/RESULT.md. Next useful question is tile occupancy for
actual SC117 routed shapes, not rediscovering grouping. Gate partial-route and
ragged-K cases against existing exact grouped outputs before full timing.

4. Hybrid parallel topology rather than another pure-PP scheduler alone.
Primary: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/getting_started/installation/gpu.xpu.inc.md
Primary: https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/api/vllm/config/parallel/
Primary: https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-05-14-elastic-expert-parallelism
Concrete options: two PP stages each with TP2; independent attention workers
with shared expert ownership; or two independent two-card replicas for multiple
users. Upstream documents XPU TP, beta PP and XCCL P2P configuration; its EP
architecture separates attention/DP from expert groups. This does NOT establish
that every EP collective/backend works well on B70. PCIe collective latency and
exported-buffer host accounting can dominate a single stream. Redistribution
also changes reductions unless deliberately preserved. First gate: CPU placement
and measured transfer-budget estimate with actual tensor/route sizes, then one
correctness-and-timing comparison. Treat concurrency throughput separately from
single-request latency; no cache reuse required.

5. Adaptive speculation with existing draft weights and a fixed verifier.
Primary: https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/llm-api/reference/EagleDecodingConfig.html
Primary: https://github.com/labscommunity/cascadia
Concrete idea: select speculative depth using current acceptance and measured
cost, or move proposal work to an otherwise idle owner while preserving hidden
parent capture. TensorRT distinguishes greedy argmax verification from sampled
rejection schemes; Cascadia provides an inspectable distributed-speculation
engine, but states TP is plumbing only. Neither is a B70/SC117 plug-in. Keep our
existing MTP weights and exact target verification; tree heads requiring training
are outside scope. N-gram/history and prompt-lookup acceleration are excluded
from the cold final suite. Local depth2 two-rank work lost on scheduling; PP4
capture-ready already exists. Gate only a materially different cost/ownership
policy, with exact accept/abort/provider-state comparison and unchanged prompts.

6. Expert/KV residency and prefetch without changing stored values.
Primary: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/63666
This is an RFC, not a proven deployable B70 implementation. It proposes using
an existing next-layer router on available hidden state to predict expert loads
without training, then overlapping transfers; actual routing must still decide
which experts compute, with a correct miss fallback. Its analysis says larger
prefill chunks touch nearly all experts, limiting expert caching for prefill.
For our already-resident four-card target, expert offload is unlikely to help
speed. More relevant extensions are more users, larger models or full-precision
KV residency. Preserve original weight/KV bytes and avoid memory-setting changes.
Gate actual route locality and transfer volume first; reject if avoided transfer
cannot exceed added prediction/copy cost. No new offload system just on promise.

7. Quantized-KV kernels: useful layout research, excluded current lane.
Primary: https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler/issues/739
Primary: https://community.intel.com/t5/Blogs/Tech-Innovation/Data-Center/Enhancing-long-context-high-concurrency-LLM-serving-on-a-32-GB/post/1751209
The Xe2 issue proposes native INT8 per-token/head KV support with register-local
dequantization; Intel's TurboQuant article describes tiled split-KV decode.
Useful transport/kernel ideas: combine conversion with loads, minimize temporary
buffers, and avoid an unfused dequantization pass. Quantized KV itself changes
values; FP8/INT8/TurboQuant are not lossless substitutes for our BF16/FP16 cache.
Owner rules prohibit silently adopting them. Record as a separate hypothetical
quality tradeoff, not an optimization to run now. The exact current-lane version
is 16-bit layout/prefetch improvement from item2, not new cache quantization.

8. Sparse attention: distinguish model-defined selection from approximation.
Primary: https://github.com/mit-han-lab/Quest
Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10774
Quest tracks per-page key min/max, estimates relevance from the query, then
loads a fixed top-K page budget. It explicitly approximates dense attention;
accuracy results do not establish identical outputs. Do not graft that page
selection onto dense or native-QSA attention as an exact speedup. Transferable
idea: compact metadata, tile-gather and resident selected pages after the model's
unchanged selection has already produced exact indices. Gate equality of selected
indices, ordering, ties and arithmetic, then compare loaded bytes and timing.
This offers a broader long-context avenue than changing PP wave size, while
retaining the native model rather than inventing a new sparse one.

9. Other models/forks as kernel stress cases, not equal-quality substitutes.
Primary: https://github.com/Torchit1/llama.cpp
Primary: https://github.com/Torchit1/llama.cpp/blob/arc-b580/docs/bonsai-arc-b580.md
Primary: https://github.com/Torchit1
Torchit1 exposes Arc B580 SYCL/XMX prompt work, GDN work and a Bonsai ternary
route. B580 is related Intel discrete hardware; scheduling/SLM/register choices
still require B70 validation. Ternary models are separately trained/quantized
models, not SC117 compressed without loss. Inspect its prompt/GDN kernel layout
against our generic runtime interfaces; retain our original arithmetic and
formats. Its recipe includes quantized KV and draft modes that do not transfer
to our cold full-precision lane. Local GDN_PREP already won and is enabled:
research/gdn-prefill-c23/RESULT.md. Prioritize a new shape/layout mechanism,
not re-testing that same preparation switch. No model download authorized here.

10. B70-specific tuning rules rather than blind generic Triton defaults.
Primary paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26118
Xe-Forge evaluates Intel Arc Pro B70 and highlights valid warp counts, GRF modes
and SLM sizing as necessary kernel-search constraints. Its reported comparisons
are against its own PyTorch/attention baselines, not our custom kernel. Useful
idea: make production shape identities drive a small valid tile/register search,
with exactness first, one fair timing pass and explicit negative records. This
is kernel tuning, not draft/model training. Start from a measured expensive site
on our current lane; do not build another general optimizer framework or launch
an unconstrained sweep while the current run is active.

Community sources and boundaries
- https://github.com/SergiioB/intel-arc-pro-b70-inference-cookbook
  Read its current decision/patch matrix, not its repository-title speed claims.
  Its README says later matched tests did not reproduce a historical MoE MTP
  result; default two-card deployment is independent servers. Its EXL3 route
  uses FP8 KV and therefore has a different quality/run identity from ours.
- https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler is the Intel-maintained B60/B70 stack;
  inspect fixes and kernels without treating moving image tags as an oracle.
- GRIMOIRE primary repository confirmed: https://github.com/doopeworld/GRIMOIRE
  Revision pinned by git ls-remote HEAD:
  c0aefd0334d016fbbd2b1adbc0be729bd6e247fe
  The three documents below are at repository ROOT, not docs/. They describe
  historical states; do not assume every old experiment is the current default.

  A. Native-format expert tiering and separate prefill/decode placement.
  https://github.com/doopeworld/GRIMOIRE/blob/c0aefd0334d016fbbd2b1adbc0be729bd6e247fe/FLASH-NEXT-TIERED.md
  Concrete design: pointer-addressed native NVFP4 expert blocks in GPU or pinned
  host storage, CPU execution of cold experts during decode alongside GPU hot
  experts, and streamed expert staging for prefill. It explicitly avoids a second
  MXFP4 quantization of existing NVFP4 weights. Useful for one/two-card capacity
  and larger contexts, not obviously faster than our fully resident quad lane.
  It also describes executing native QSA block selections through masked XMX
  attention instead of sparse gathers: directly relevant to exact selected-KV
  layout research. Caveats: native NVFP4 is not our SC117 quant; CPU/GPU sums and
  BF16 staging can change arithmetic; same text on one 6K prompt is not bitwise
  state parity. Its projected speeds are estimates, not local evidence. Minimal
  gate: exact unchanged route/index selection and dequant bytes, then one selected
  attention/expert operator state test before considering a capacity experiment.

  B. Output-row TP as a different hybrid topology building block.
  https://github.com/doopeworld/GRIMOIRE/blob/c0aefd0334d016fbbd2b1adbc0be729bd6e247fe/HANDOFF-2026-08-27-PP-TP-COMPLETE.md
  The documented two-process TP splits projection OUTPUT rows then all-gathers,
  rather than splitting the dot-product reduction. Potentially preserve each
  output's original reduction order while combining TP within PP stages; useful
  contrast to reduction-heavy TP. But this historical version retains FULL
  weights in both processes and replicates MoE/batched-prefill work. It is not
  memory-sharded TP or an established quad speedup. Correctness there is a short
  prompt with a few matching tokens, not our full-state gate. First assess actual
  packed-weight replication and gather traffic before coding; then exact-state
  test a production projection. Prefix-cache work in this document is outside
  our cold-response benchmark scope.

  C. MTP restoration scope must include canonical state, not just fast drafts.
  https://github.com/doopeworld/GRIMOIRE/blob/c0aefd0334d016fbbd2b1adbc0be729bd6e247fe/FAST-MTP-RESTORE-2026-09-04.md
  Concrete lesson: revert compact/zero draft-KV experiments unless exact replay
  state survives transitions; keep checkpoint synchronization tied to verified
  execution. The historical restoration names an unchanged mtp_draft function
  and disables a graph path after device loss. Its fast recipe excludes the
  later EXACT_VERIFY flag and reports coherent output; the restoration itself
  was not rebuilt or GPU-tested before handoff. Those facts do NOT prove target
  verification is absent, but they also cannot establish our exactness standard.
  Transfer only a source-audited bounded snapshot/restore mechanism preserving
  every native mutable allocation. Local bulk replay already failed a following
  request transition: research/bulk-replay/RESULT.md. Reproduce that seam first;
  never replace it with a coherence test or disable our verifier for speed.

Highest-value intake shortlist: small-batch XMX data reuse with unchanged math;
full-precision selected-KV layout/attention occupancy; actual-shape expert tile
specialization; hybrid topology/concurrency. All remain hypotheses until local
matched correctness and timing. External exactness labels are not our gate.
