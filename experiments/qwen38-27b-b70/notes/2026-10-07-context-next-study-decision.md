## Branch B: registered historical-state fallback

**Next bounded experiment: eight fresh trials on t01-clinic and t02-theatre.** Cross bookkeeping method (archive/quoted) with answer access (source-only/history). All four conditions must use the *same new history engine*, not old semantic results as controls. Both access modes save and validate exactly the same actual-state snapshots and pay the same persistence overhead. Source-only uses the frozen semantic answer instruction and denies `state_at`; history changes instruction and tool permission. This estimates the combined instruction/access policy, not a pure change in storage or a guaranteed isolated instruction effect.

Define conditions:

| Code | Bookkeeping | Answer access |
|---|---|---|
| AS | Archive | Source-only |
| QH | Quoted | History |
| QS | Quoted | Source-only |
| AH | Archive | History |

Freeze the order **t01: AS, QH, QS, AH; t02: AH, QS, QH, AS**. The second document reverses the first order. Each condition has mean within-document position 2.5; each factor is balanced across the two documents. Every cell ingests the document afresh. Do not share or restore one ingestion across answer modes: that would introduce an unimplemented resume/fork protocol and a different cost denominator. Model variability in fresh ingestion remains visible and must be reported.

Finish all eight cells despite bounded model/protocol failures, keeping the unchanged caps and all costs. Do not stop early merely because one condition performs badly. Infrastructure failure stops execution and leaves the remaining fixed rows explicitly unstarted. No summary arm, generated sparse cases or parameter search is needed in this block.

Primary outputs, per document and per condition:

* Exact current answers out of 8, historical balances out of 8, and historical ownership joins out of 8; missing/null answers remain wrong. Also report strict 24/24 completion and all twelve checkpoint states.
* Wrong/missing/extra archive state, quoted extraction errors/refusals, and source evidence for the owner at the requested close. A history lookup faithfully returning a wrong accepted value is an ingestion failure, not corrected state.
* Successful distinct retrievals by action, repeated/cached retrieval calls, answer-call exhaustion, answer-phase time/tokens, and total elapsed time including ingestion and snapshot persistence. Show failures and their spent cost rather than computing a success-only speed average.
* For exact cold pairs only, within-document history-versus-source total elapsed and answer-phase costs for each arm, plus archive-versus-quoted comparisons within each mode. Do not pool these distinct estimands into one speed figure.

Freeze a modest continuation rule before these eight trials: t03/t04 receive the same four conditions only if evidence is complete and auditable, every history cell is exact at all checkpoints and 24/24 answers, and history does not lower either current or temporal answer accuracy for either arm on either initial document. In addition, require either (a) at least one temporal-answer improvement over its same-arm source-only counterpart, or (b) one arm that is fully exact in both modes and obtains at least 10% less *total* elapsed with history on **both** documents, with all relevant calls known cold. This is a development continuation signal, not statistical significance or a promotion gate. The threshold and documents must not change after outputs arrive.

If the continuation rule fails, stop after eight and diagnose. In particular, uniformly exact source-only results plus no consistent cost saving provide no reason for a larger tool matrix. Ingestion errors that survive in saved state call for a separate source-extraction study; ownership errors require source/ticket reasoning because `state_at` stores counter values, not historical ownership. Do not silently add ownership state, repair snapshots from references or change prompts/caps within this study.

If the rule passes, the next separately frozen block is t03 **QS, AH, AS, QH** and t04 **QH, AS, AH, QS**. Across all four documents, each condition occupies every position once. These remaining cases were authored and annotated before model output, but are still development cases, not a held-out benchmark. Preserve first-block and extension results separately, including the selection rule.
