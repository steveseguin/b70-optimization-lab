Pick your setup

Models

Before you tune anything, you pick a model — and that choice sets your ceiling. The single most important thing to understand: a model's speed is set by its active parameters, not its total size. That's why a "26B" model can be five times faster than a "27B" one.

The short version

  • Total parameters decide how much VRAM the model needs. Active parameters decide how fast it decodes.
  • Dense models use all their weights per token. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models use only a slice — so they're fast for their size.
  • A name like A4B or A3B tells you the active size (≈4B or 3B active) — that's your speed hint.
  • Fit first (does it load with room for context?), then speed, then quality and features (vision, tools).

Dense vs mixture-of-experts

A dense model runs every one of its weights for every token. A 27B dense model does ~27B parameters' worth of work per token — accurate, but heavy on the memory reads that set decode speed.

A mixture-of-experts model is split into many "experts," and a router picks only a few per token. It might hold 30B parameters total but activate just 3–4B on any given token. You pay the VRAM of the big model but the speed of a small one. That's the trick behind the fastest models on modest hardware.

Reading a model name

Qwen3.8-27B-A3B: 27B total (VRAM), ~3B active (speed). Gemma-4-26B-A4B: 26B total, ~4B active. Nemotron-30B-A3B: 30B total, ~3B active. When you see A#B, that's the number that predicts how fast it will feel. No A#B? Assume dense — total = active.

The VRAM math

Whatever the design, the model's weights must fit in memory, and you need room left for the KV cache (your context) on top. A rough single-card budget on a 32 GiB B70:

  • Weights: total params × bits-per-weight ÷ 8. A 27B model at 4-bit (Q4) ≈ 15–18 GB; at 8-bit (Q8) ≈ 27–30 GB. See Quantization.
  • KV cache: grows with context; can be several GB to 16 GB at very long context.
  • Rule of thumb: keep weights + expected KV under ~30 GB on a 32 GiB card. If a Q8 model is tight, drop to Q4 or add a card.

How to choose

  1. Match to your cards. One B70? A fast MoE (Gemma-4-26B) or a Q4 27B. More cards unlock bigger models and speculative records.
  2. Match to your job. Coding and agents want tools and speed; document work wants long context; image tasks need a vision model (Qwen3.8 reads pictures).
  3. Then optimize. Once the model fits and runs, the other eight guides are how you make it faster.
What to be aware of

A high headline number can hide caveats: it may be a research lane with unresolved determinism, a narrow benchmark shape, or a different card count than yours. It may also be a model that's fast but weaker at your task. And new weights we're still bringing to the board (e.g. Ornith, Nemotron, LFM2.5) have no B70 numbers yet — we don't guess them. Always check the evidence link and the card count behind a rate before believing it applies to you.

Recommendation

For most people on a single B70: start with Gemma-4-26B if you want raw speed, or Qwen3.8-27B Q4_K_M if you want vision, tools, long context, and a fully quality-checked package. Get it running, then come back and tune.
